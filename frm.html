Risk modelling projects

Three Excel models built for the FRM course: market risk, consumer credit risk and bank liquidity risk. All calculations are formula-linked to the data sheets.

1. Market risk: VaR on a 9-stock PSX portfolio

Ten years of daily PSX prices, the KSE-100 and KIBOR tenors (SBP EasyData) were aligned chronologically; log returns were built at daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly frequency. The project covers 50+ tasks: moments, parametric VaR (86.5/92/96.5/98.5%), historical VaR, betas against KSE-100 and 6M KIBOR, correlation and variance-covariance matrices, portfolio VaR, VaR decomposition (standalone, marginal, component), and multi-asset scenarios across currencies, commodities, international indices and benchmark rates.

Individual σ and VaR use each stock's full history; betas use the common portfolio window (GCIL listed Nov 2022). VaR = mean − z·σ.

Diversification works: the portfolio's daily 98.5% historical VaR (−2.65%) is far smaller than the weighted standalone VaRs, and a beta of 0.70 makes it defensive relative to the index. The 6M KIBOR beta is slightly negative (−0.006), matching the usual inverse link between rates and equities.

2. Credit risk: consumer lending scorecard

A scoring model for car loans, personal loans, running finance and credit cards, with 60%+ of fields taken directly from a Pakistani bank's consumer application form, as the brief required.

  • Six weighted categories: financial capacity 28%, credit bureau and banking history 22%, employment 20%, personal 12%, product and collateral 12%, references and digital signals 6%.
  • Output: a score out of 100 mapped to a 12-grade rating and a lending decision, with 8 key risk indicators flagged green, yellow or red (debt-burden ratio, eCIB, loan-to-income, LTV, savings rate and others).
  • 13 auto-checked hard stops override the score. Examples: debt-burden ratio above the SBP 50% cap (Prudential Regulation R-4), an active eCIB default (Annexure CF-1), age and tenor limits, and car-loan LTV above 70%.

3. Liquidity risk: LCR, bank-run stress and maturity GAP

  • Basel III LCR under three inflow caps (74%, 51% and 24% of outflows) gives 400%, 212% and 137%. All three are compliant, but an oil-shock scenario would compress them sharply.
  • Bank-run stress test: with liquidity conversion factors of 1 / 0.98 / 0.6 / 0, the bank survives withdrawals of Rs 105m and Rs 295m but fails from Rs 695m. The critical withdrawal is Rs 386.5m, or 40.7% of deposits. Tighter factors bring failure forward to Rs 300m.
  • Maturity GAP: every bucket up to 12 months is positive, but the 240–360-month bucket shows a gap of −2.77m that turns cumulative MAGAP negative. That is a structural long-end funding mismatch, and the model sets out the fixes.
